# Urban Mobility & Traffic Analytics — Business Insights

## 1. Executive Summary

The analysis of 168 traffic records shows clear variations in congestion,
speed, and travel time across different days and time periods.

The most challenging traffic conditions are concentrated during the evening
period, while early-morning periods show comparatively lighter traffic.

---

## 2. Key Business Insights

### Insight 1 — Evening is the critical traffic period

Average congestion reaches **56.00% at 20:00**, the highest among the
analyzed time periods.

Average travel time reaches **26.37 minutes at 18:00**, while average speed
falls to **21.57 km/h**.

**Business Meaning:**  
The evening period represents the most challenging traffic window in the
analyzed dataset.

**Recommendation:**  
Traffic management teams can prioritize monitoring and congestion-control
measures during the evening peak period.

---

### Insight 2 — Wednesday has the highest average congestion

Wednesday records the highest average daily congestion at **26.96%**.

It also has the lowest average daily speed at **29.04 km/h**.

**Business Meaning:**  
Wednesday shows the strongest combination of higher congestion and lower
average speed among the analyzed days.

**Recommendation:**  
Traffic monitoring can pay additional attention to Wednesday traffic
conditions.

---

### Insight 3 — Sunday has the lightest traffic conditions

Sunday records the lowest average congestion at **12.08%**.

It also has the highest average speed at **31.83 km/h** and the shortest
average travel time at approximately **18.81 minutes**.

**Business Meaning:**  
The analyzed data shows substantially lighter traffic conditions on Sunday.

**Recommendation:**  
Traffic resources and monitoring intensity could be adjusted according to
the lower traffic demand observed on weekends.

---

### Insight 4 — Congestion is strongly associated with lower speed

The correlation between congestion and speed is **-0.841**.

**Business Meaning:**  
As congestion increases, speed tends to decrease strongly in this dataset.

**Recommendation:**  
Speed and congestion can be monitored together as complementary indicators
for identifying deteriorating traffic conditions.

---

### Insight 5 — Higher congestion is strongly associated with longer travel time

The correlation between congestion and travel time is **+0.916**.

**Business Meaning:**  
Higher congestion levels are strongly associated with longer travel times.

**Recommendation:**  
Traffic dashboards can use congestion as an early indicator for potential
increases in travel time.

---

### Insight 6 — City and Urban areas show different patterns

| Area | Avg Congestion | Avg Speed | Avg Travel Time |
|------|---------------:|----------:|----------------:|
| City | 22.18% | 29.60 km/h | 20.94 min |
| Urban | 24.02% | 30.05 km/h | 20.16 min |

Urban records show slightly higher average congestion, while City records
show slightly higher average travel time.

**Business Meaning:**  
Traffic characteristics differ between the two analyzed area categories.

**Recommendation:**  
Traffic planning and monitoring can consider area-specific patterns instead
of applying the same strategy everywhere.

---

### Insight 7 — The highest individual congestion record occurs during the evening

The highest individual congestion value is **67%**, recorded on **Tuesday
at 18:00 in the City area**.

The same record has a speed of **20 km/h** and travel time of approximately
**28.08 minutes**.

**Business Meaning:**  
This record represents one of the most severe traffic conditions observed
in the dataset.

**Recommendation:**  
Individual peak records can be investigated alongside average trends to
identify specific periods requiring closer traffic monitoring.

---

## 3. Overall Business Takeaway

The analysis indicates that **time of day is an important factor in the
observed traffic patterns**, with evening periods showing higher congestion,
lower speeds, and longer travel times.

The strong relationships between congestion, speed, and travel time also
suggest that these metrics should be monitored together when evaluating
traffic conditions.

This analysis can support data-driven traffic monitoring, peak-period
planning, and area-level traffic management decisions.

---

## 4. Important Note

This analysis is based on the available traffic dataset and describes
patterns and relationships observed within the data.

Correlation results indicate association and **do not by themselves prove
causation**.
